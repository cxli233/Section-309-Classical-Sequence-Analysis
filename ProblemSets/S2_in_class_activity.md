# Session 2 in-class activities 
## Q1 
Identify a database that you used or will use for your research. 
Explore the database and answer the following questions. 
If you do not have a database in mind, please explore [the Arabidopsis database](https://www.arabidopsis.org/).

1. What kind of data is housed in the database? 
2. What species are accepted by the database? 
3. What tools are available at the database? 
Hint: Common tools include search, genome browser, BLAST, structure viewer, for example. 
4. Check how you can cite the database. Is the database well-cited by your field? 
5. Can you tell if the database is well-maintained and human curated? 

## Q2
Imagine you are working on the biotechnology for cheesemaking. 
A committee member of yours asked you to look into "chymosin" for your work, but you don't know anything about it. 

1. Go to NCBI and put "chymosin" in the search bar. What is chymosin? What does it do?
2. Now try running the search in the Protein database within NCBI. 
Figure out how to confine your search to RefSeq. How many entries do you have?
Click on "Tree" in the top right corner of the page. Which organisms or kinds of organisms have chymosin?

## Q3
Now try searching for chymosin at EMBL and look at the protein results. 
Click on "UniRef100", which combines 100% identical sequences and their subfragments into a single entry. 
How are they different from NCBI?

## Q4 
Now go to UniProt and search for chymosin. Click on the entry "P00794". 

1. Which organism is this protein from? 
2. How many experimentally determined structures exist for this entry? What are the PDB Identifiers? 
3. How many Alphafold predicted structures exist for this entry?

## Q5
Now go through Q2-Q4 again, but pick something relevant to your research project
(or a project you would like to have or found interesting in a previous course). 
Look for something in which the databases have a gene sequence, protein sequence, 
and at least one structure, predicted or experimentally determined.

Briefly describe your protein of interest in words. 
Save the protein sequence in a fasta file.
