# Session 10 in-class activities 
## Q1a
Head to [PDB](https://www.rcsb.org/) and search for entry 102M. 
Read the description of this entry. 

1. What species and protein is this?
2. What experimental method was used to produce this structure? 
3. What ligand(s) are part of this structure?

## Q1b
Download the structure of entry 102M. Open it in ChimeraX. 
You can click `Open` in the upper right to select your file.
You can change the background color to white by clicking `White` in the upper panel.
I like the "soft" lighting scheme. You can enable that by clicking `Soft` in the upper panel.

1. Click `Select` -> `Chain`. How many chains of protein are in this structure? Select them. 
2. Click `Action` -> `Color` and choose a color you like. I like hot pink.  
3. Click `Select` -> `Structure` -> `Ligand`. Can you tell what is being selected? 
4. Click `Action` -> `Color` and choose a color you like. I like cornflower blue to contrast with hot pink.
5. Click `Action` -> `Color` -> `By Heteroatom`. Now non-carbon atoms are colored separately.  
6. Click `Select` -> `Structure` -> `Ions`. Can you tell what is being selected? 
7. Click `Action` -> `Color` and choose a color you like. I like light sea green to go with cornflower blue.
8. Click `Tools` -> `Sequence` -> `Show Sequence Viewer` and select `Show` in the pop-up window.
You will see that the alpha helices are highlighted yellow. How many helices are there? 
9. Rotate the structure and explore it by holding the left click of your mouse and drag it around.
Can you find the critical histidine (His93) that binds the heme? 
10. Click the `Right Mouse` tab and click `Select`. Right click on His93 to select it. 
11. Click `Action` -> `Color` and choose a color you like. I like orange here. 
12. Click `Snapshot` in the `Home` tab. An image will be saved to your machine. 
Locate it and give it a descriptive file name. Upload to D2L. 
13. Click `Save` and save your session to your machine. 
You can now close this session by clicking `File` -> `Close Session`.

## Q2b
Download the structure of entry 7FDO. Open it in ChimeraX. 
Change the background to white and select soft lighting. 
Locate the `Models` window at the lower right. 
Click the grey triangle next to 7FDO. Uncheck "missing structure". 
(Part of the protein is missing in the model, likely unstructured regions that cannot be crystallized.) 

1. Click `Select` -> `Chain`. How many chains of protein are in this structure? Select chain A (EGL3). 
2. Click `Action` -> `Color` and choose a color you like. I like hot pink. 
3. Do the same for chain B  (CPC) and choose a color you like. I like cornflower blue to contrast with hot pink.
4. Click `Select` -> `Chemistry` -> `Protein`. What is being selected? 
5. Go to `Molecular Display` tab and click `Show` in the `Surfaces` panel. How well do the 2 proteins fit together?  
6. Click `Actions` -> `Surface` -> `Transparency` -> `60%`. Can you see the cartoon under the surface? 
7. Click `Tools` -> `Sequence` -> `Show Sequence Viewer`. 
Select both chains by holding down `Ctrl` for PC and `Cmd` for Mac before clicking `Show` in the pop-up window.
You will see that the alpha helices are highlighted in yellow, and the beta strands are highlighted blue.
How many beta strands are in this structure? 
8. According to the literature, the binding surface of chain B has the following sequence motif: 
`[D/E]xxLxxxxxxLxxxxxxLxxxR`, where `x` is any amino acid. Find and highlight the motif in the sequence viewer.
Change its color to light sea green. Is the motif at the binding interface? 
9. Click `Snapshot` in the `Home` tab. An image will be saved to your machine. 
Locate it and give it a descriptive file name. Upload to D2L. 
10. Click `Save` and save your session to your machine. 
You can now close this session by clicking `File` -> `Close Session`.

## Q4
Search for camel chymosin on Uniprot.
You will see that it has an X-ray structure and an AlphaFold predicted structure. 
Download both from the respective databases. 

1. Load the crystal structure (entry 4AA9) to ChimeraX first.
2. In the same session, open the AlphaFold predicted structure (entry AF-Q9GK11-F1-model_v6). Hide the atoms. 
3. Click `Tools` -> `Structure Analysis` -> `Matchmaker` and select 4AA9 as the reference. 
Are there structural features presented in the AlphaFold prediction but are absent in the experimentally determined structure?
Can you speculate why that is the case?
Hint: Check the sequence viewer. Are there any part(s) of the protein that are missing from the crystal structure? 
4. Highlight the first 70 or so amino acids in the AlphaFold prediction (using sequence viewer). Hide the cartoon.
Save a snapshot and upload to D2L. You may close the session after saving it.
